# Discovery plan — research participants not yet recruited

## Research questions

- When and why did a user last customize a theme?
- What tools, steps and time did they use, and where did they give up?
- Do they need matching colors on **two or more applications**, or simply want independent attractive themes?
- Would one app-specific color edit need to stay isolated from other apps?
- Does preview accuracy or safe installation outweigh cross-app convenience?

## Recruitment

Target 12-15 users who changed a theme within the last six months. Include 4-5 users who customize multiple apps, 4-5 who download ready-made themes, and 3-5 who create themes. Avoid exclusive recruitment from only developer communities. Do not present a cross-app theme editor as the assumed solution in the screener.

## Interview (25-30 minutes)

1. What is the last application you customized? Show, if available, how you did it.
2. Walk me through the last change you made. What did you expect and what actually happened?
3. Which other apps have you customized? What, if anything, did you try to match?
4. Did any tool or step feel risky, too complicated, or tedious? What did you do instead?
5. How did you know your theme would work after an app update?
6. What would you sacrifice first: precision, speed, app coverage, or easy installation?
7. After the behavioral questions, show a neutral prototype; ask the participant to perform a task without leading them.

## Evidence log per participant

Anonymous ID; relevant apps; last action/date; demonstrable steps; pain intensity; workaround; time and errors on prototype; verbatim anonymized quote; counterevidence; willingness-to-pay behavior (if tested). Do not count intentions as observed purchases.

## Decision gate

Continue beyond G1 only if users demonstrate a repeated costly workflow or clear drop in task friction relative to current tools. Aesthetic preference alone is insufficient.
